# Design inspiration and UX principles (researched 2026-11-16)

## Real products and design references
| Reference | What to take from it |
|---|---|
| [Wysa: AI self-help](https://www.wysa.com/ai-self-help) | An **always-visible SOS button** that leads to local crisis lines, plus background detection of distress. This matches our red banner and two-layer check. |
| [Wysa chat redesign (Emmanuel Bliss, Contra)](https://contra.com/p/N7voZM7L-wysa-mental-health-chat-redesign) | Remove visual clutter from the chat. Small friction adds up for users who are already overwhelmed. |
| [Woebot conversation-design case study (UX Writing Hub)](https://uxwritinghub.com/woebot-case-study-in-conversation-design-for-mental-health-products/) | Mostly **buttons plus some free text**, a friendly tone, and an SOS option that must stay prominent. |
| [freud.ai UI kit (Dribbble)](https://dribbble.com/shots/22020229--freud-ai-Mental-Health-AI-Chatbot-Platform-Mobile-UIUX-Care) | A full chatbot design system including **dark mode** (compare our direction C). |
| [Mirren chat (Kombai gallery)](https://kombai.com/gallery/inspirations/chat-bot-ios-23/) | A soft grey-white chat surface with teal user bubbles (compare our direction B). |
| [Student services app (Dribbble)](https://dribbble.com/shots/24359902-Student-services-app-design) | A home screen with the services students use most. Useful for the welcome page. |

## Principles to apply
- **Crisis help within 2 taps, always visible:** red banner/SOS on every screen ([Boundev](https://www.boundev.ai/blog/ux-design-for-mental-health-apps); [UX Planet youth site lessons](https://uxplanet.org/lessons-learnt-from-redesigning-a-youth-mental-health-service-website-b3de127f0cfb)).
- **Off-hours alternatives:** when the unit is closed, show 24-hour resources (UX Planet). Our night direction C does this.
- **Short, simple text in crisis:** people in crisis process information less well, so avoid walls of text ([Clearleft](https://clearleft.com/thinking/designing-for-people-in-crisis)).
- **Trust is honesty:** say clearly it is a program, not a person ([Wysa / EMHIC](https://emhicglobal.com/?p=12203)).
- **Privacy visible in the interface,** not hidden in a policy page ([Biz4Group 2026](https://www.biz4group.com/blog/best-practices-in-mental-health-design)). Our note under the booking form does this.
- **Accessibility:** adjustable font size and high contrast ([Sigma Software](https://sigma.software/insights/the-importance-of-user-experience-in-mental-health-apps)). Wysa follows WCAG 2.1.
- **No guilt mechanics:** avoid streaks or pressure to come back daily (Boundev).
- **Involve mental-health professionals** in design and evaluation ([JMIR 2025](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e67114)).
